Adaptation is the process by which traits get shaped — over generations, by selective pressure — to fit the environment an organism actually lives in. A polar bear's coat, a cactus's water storage, a gut bacterium's resistance to an antibiotic: each is a solution tuned to a specific set of conditions. The key word is *specific*. Adaptation is always adaptation *to* something; it is never fitness in the abstract.

That's the trap when the environment changes. A trait that was advantageous can turn into a liability — the same specialization that made an organism (or a company) dominant in one niche is often what sinks it when the niche shifts. Kodak was superbly adapted to a film world, and that very fit slowed its move to digital. Adaptation also lags: it optimizes for the environment of the recent past, not the one that's arriving.

**Why it matters:** ask what environment a strength was built for, and whether that environment still exists. Durable advantage comes less from being well-adapted today than from being able to re-adapt faster than conditions change.
