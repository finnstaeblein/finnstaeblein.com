Adaptation is the process by which, over many generations, traits get shaped to fit the environment an organism lives in — selective pressure favoring whatever improves survival and reproduction. It is always adaptation *to* something specific; it is never fitness in the abstract.

Selection also has a hard horizon: it only acts on traits up to roughly the end of an organism's reproductive age. What happens to a body after it can no longer reproduce has no bearing on how much offspring it leaves — so nothing weeds out traits that only cause trouble late in life. This "selection shadow" is much of why organisms senesce and break down after their reproductive peak: the pressure that shaped them simply stops paying attention.

And because adaptation is always to a *specific* environment, a trait that was advantageous can turn into a liability when conditions change. The same specialization that made an organism — or a company like Kodak — dominant in one niche is often what sinks it when the niche shifts. Adaptation optimizes for the environment of the recent past, not the one that's arriving.

**Why it matters:** ask what environment a strength was built for, and whether that environment still exists. Durable advantage comes less from being well-adapted today than from being able to re-adapt faster than conditions change.
